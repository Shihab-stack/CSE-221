Problem G: Sorting Again??

Each student has a unique ID and a mark. Order students by decreasing mark;
when marks are equal, the lower ID comes first. Find the minimum number of
arbitrary swaps of two students needed to reach that order, and print the
ordered student records. The number of swaps is the minimum over all possible
sequences of swaps.

Input
The first line contains T (1 <= T <= 100). Each test case consists of:
- N (1 <= N <= 1000);
- N distinct student IDs (1 <= ID <= 1000);
- N marks (1 <= mark <= 1000), corresponding to those IDs in order.

Output
For each test case, print:
Minimum swaps: X
followed by N lines in the required order, each formatted as:
ID: student_id Mark: mark

Example
Input:
2
7
7 4 9 3 2 5 1
40 50 50 20 10 10 10
3
1 2 3
40 59 10
Output:
Minimum swaps: 4
ID: 4 Mark: 50
ID: 9 Mark: 50
ID: 7 Mark: 40
ID: 3 Mark: 20
ID: 1 Mark: 10
ID: 2 Mark: 10
ID: 5 Mark: 10
Minimum swaps: 1
ID: 2 Mark: 59
ID: 1 Mark: 40
ID: 3 Mark: 10

Forbidden keywords in the solution: sort, open, file, creat(, fstream, thread,
process, system(, exec(.
